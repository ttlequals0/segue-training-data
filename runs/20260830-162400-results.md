## Feed overlap did not inflate the score

8 of the 17 training feeds are shows that also appear in the benchmark
corpus, though never the same episodes. Splitting the 12 ad-bearing
episodes by whether their feed appeared in training:

| Split | Episodes | Mean F1 |
|---|---|---|
| Feed seen in training | 6 | 0.650 |
| Feed never trained on | 6 | 0.617 |

A 0.033 gap on six episodes per side is noise. The score reflects
generalization rather than memorized shows.

## Where it loses

Every point of the gap is recall: 23 false negatives against 9 false
positives. Precision is already close to the hosted tier.

| Bucket | Detection rate |
|---|---|
| Short ads (<30s) | 0.25 (n=8) |
| Medium ads (30 to 90s) | 0.47 (n=15) |
| Long ads (>=90s) | 0.68 (n=28) |
| Pre-roll | 0.47 (n=15) |
| Mid-roll | 0.68 (n=25) |
| Post-roll | 0.36 (n=11) |

The mechanism is span merging. The model fuses adjacent spots into one
span, and every other ad in that block becomes a false negative; its own
reason fields name the merged sponsors. Boundary bias agrees: start bias
-7.5s and end bias +4.9s, so predictions extend outward past the real ad.
Boundary jitter and hard negatives are aimed at exactly this.

Weakest episodes: tosh-show 0.250, the-tim-dillon-show 0.250,
the-brilliant-idiots 0.400. Strongest: on-air-with-dan-and-alex2 1.000,
crime-junkie 0.857, glt1412515089 0.857, security-now-audio 0.833.

## Caveats

- Scored against the live system prompt (sha256 7652e74a), which is the one
  the training data used. The published multi-model table used the frozen
  2026-08 snapshot (sha256 1030b29e), so a strict leaderboard comparison
  needs a rerun with `--snapshot`.
- One trial only, so the 0.000 F1 stdev carries no information.
- One window took 901s against a 20s p99. The cause is unknown and worth
  watching on the next run.
